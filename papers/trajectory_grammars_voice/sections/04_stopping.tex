% §4 Stopping Rules for Outcome Prediction
The grammar of Section~\ref{sec:grammar} turns a conversation into a walk over $Q$ that emits typed observations at every turn.
A decision layer must convert this growing evidence into an action --- intervene, escalate, flag, or wait --- at a time that is early enough to matter and honest about its own error rates.
This section defines that layer: an outcome-class posterior maintained per turn, a sequential stopping rule over the posterior, and cost-asymmetric decision boundaries derived from the banking cost model.
The centerpiece is the Trajectory Stopping Theorem, which bounds the expected decision time at a prescribed confidence and subsumes the fixed-threshold triggering of learned early-failure detectors as a special case.

\subsection{From Grammar Emissions to an Outcome Posterior}
\label{subsec:posterior}
At turn $k$ the monitor observes the partial transcript $x_{1:k}$ together with the typed tool-event log (tool-call, tool-fail, endpoint-error, user-bargein, disclosure) defined in Section~\ref{sec:grammar}.
Together these channels induce the outcome-class posterior $p(\theta \mid x_{1:k})$ over the terminal set $T = \{\textit{contained}, \textit{abandoned}, \textit{handoff}, \textit{compliance\_escape}\}$.
The posterior is deliberately abstract: any calibrated estimator over grammar emissions suffices, and every guarantee below attaches to the stopping layer rather than to a particular model.
That partial trajectories carry outcome signal at all is established for offline evaluation pruning \citep{earlyeval2026} and for text-chat failure forecasting \citep{nourbakhsh2026tracer}; our contribution is a live decision layer with guarantees, not another estimator.
Two design constraints come from our published results rather than from new evidence.
First, text-only turn signals are near chance on Spanish hard cuts \citep{garcia2026asking}, so the posterior must condition on the multi-channel emission stream, not on the transcript alone.
Second, the cycle depth $D(\sigma)$ of the grammar is its drift statistic, and it enters the posterior as a covariate: each return to a previously visited non-terminal state shifts probability mass toward the action classes.
Deep tool-loop cycles are precisely where banking conversations drift, and the $k$-step analysis of cascade agents makes the cost of such cycles formal \citep{garcia2026latencyfloor}.

\subsection{Sequential Stopping over the Posterior}
\label{subsec:rule}
The sequential probability ratio test (SPRT) is the canonical likelihood-ratio procedure with type-I and type-II error control, recently transposed to early-stopping of LLM reasoning paths \citep{consol2025}.
We transpose it once more, from reasoning paths to conversation turns.

\begin{definition}[Sequential outcome decision rule]
\label{def:rule}
Fix the benign class $\theta_0 = \textit{contained}$ and, for each action class $\theta_a \in T \setminus \{\theta_0\}$, let
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:llr}
  \Lambda_k^{a} \;=\; \log \frac{p(x_{1:k} \mid \theta_a)}{p(x_{1:k} \mid \theta_0)}
\end{equation}
denote the log-likelihood ratio of the observed emission stream under $\theta_a$ versus $\theta_0$.
With target false-alarm rate $\alpha_a$ and miss rate $\beta_a$ for class $\theta_a$, set the upper and lower boundaries
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:boundaries}
  A_a = \log \frac{1-\beta_a}{\alpha_a}, \qquad B_a = \log \frac{\beta_a}{1-\alpha_a}.
\end{equation}
The decision time is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:kdec}
  k_{\mathrm{dec}} \;=\; \min\Bigl(\;
    \min\{k : \exists\, a,\; \Lambda_k^{a} \geq A_a \;\text{(declare } \theta_a)\},\;\;
    \min\{k : \forall\, a,\; \Lambda_k^{a} \leq B_a \;\text{(declare } \theta_0)\},\;\;
    k_{\mathrm{term}}
  \Bigr),
\end{equation}
where $k_{\mathrm{term}}$ is the turn at which the grammar walk reaches a terminal state and the outcome is observed directly.
\end{definition}

The rule is one-sided per action class and jointly conservative: declaring \emph{no action} requires every class-wise ratio to fall below its lower boundary, so a conversation cannot be written off as contained while any action hypothesis retains evidence.
This asymmetry is deliberate and is priced in Section~\ref{subsec:economics}.

\subsection{The Trajectory Stopping Theorem}
\label{subsec:theorem}
We now state the guarantee under the standard Wald conditions, adapted to the grammar.
Assume (i) emissions are conditionally independent given the terminal class, and (ii) the expected per-turn log-likelihood-ratio increment is strictly separated: under $\theta_a$ it is at least $\mu_1^{a} > 0$, and under $\theta_0$ at most $\mu_0^{a} < 0$, for every action class $a$.

\begin{theorem}[Trajectory Stopping]
\label{thm:stopping}
Under conditions (i)--(ii), the decision rule of Definition~\ref{def:rule} satisfies, for every action class $a$:
\begin{align}
  \Pr\bigl[\text{declare } \theta_0 \mid \theta_a\bigr] &\leq \beta_a,
  &
  \Pr\bigl[\text{declare } \theta_a \mid \theta_0\bigr] &\leq \alpha_a,
  \label{eq:errors}\\
  \mathbb{E}\bigl[k_{\mathrm{dec}} \mid \theta_a\bigr]
    &\leq \frac{(1-\beta_a)\, A_a + \beta_a\, B_a}{\mu_1^{a}},
  &
  \mathbb{E}\bigl[k_{\mathrm{dec}} \mid \theta_0\bigr]
    &\leq \frac{\alpha_a\, A_a + (1-\alpha_a)\, B_a}{|\mu_0^{a}|}.
  \label{eq:times}
\end{align}
Consequently, choosing $(\alpha_a, \beta_a)$ from the banking cost model yields an expected decision time
$\mathbb{E}[k_{\mathrm{dec}}] \leq k^{\ast}(\delta, c)$
at confidence at least $1-\delta$, where $\delta$ bounds the total error budget across classes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Under the true class, the log-likelihood ratio \eqref{eq:llr} is a martingale in the turn index, since the emission at turn $k$ is conditionally independent of the past given the class.
Optional stopping at $k_{\mathrm{dec}}$ preserves the martingale property because $k_{\mathrm{dec}}$ is a stopping time and the walk terminates almost surely by reaching a terminal state.
The error bounds \eqref{eq:errors} follow because stopping above $A_a$ under $\theta_0$ requires the martingale to cross the level that $\alpha_a$ tail mass permits, and symmetrically below $B_a$ under $\theta_a$.
The time bounds \eqref{eq:times} are Wald's first identity applied to the stopped ratio: the expected total log-ratio at stopping equals the expected per-turn increment times the expected number of turns, and the stopped value is confined between the boundaries up to the standard excess-over-boundary term, which the idealized continuous treatment absorbs into the inequalities.
Dividing the boundary distance by the per-turn increment $|\mu|$ converts information to turns, giving \eqref{eq:times}.
\end{proof}

The assumption we cannot discharge is per-turn independence of emissions; it holds to the extent that the grammar's typed transitions, not hidden user state, drive the observation stream.
Where the emission likelihood is unknown and the posterior is learned, likelihood ratios are unavailable in exact form.
The honest sequential alternative is an anytime-valid confidence sequence over the learned posterior, which retains error control under optional stopping at the price of wider early boundaries; we adopt this variant in the evaluation protocol of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} and report both operating curves.
Wald-type SPRT remains the reference point because its boundaries are explicit and its expected stopping time is optimal among tests with the same error rates.

\subsection{Floor Composition: Early Enough to Act}
\label{subsec:floors}
A turn index is not an intervention guarantee, so we convert $k_{\mathrm{dec}}$ to wall-clock using the published floor arithmetic.
The per-turn decision latency $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{decide}}$ of the monitor must fit inside the inter-turn budget that the onset floor leaves unfilled \citep{garcia2026latencyfloor}, and the measured decision latencies of the Spanish interim-transcript pipeline (p99 178--268~ms, cloud) show this budget exists in production \citep{garcia2026asking}.
Executing the intervention is a second bound: taking the floor from an agent mid-utterance is a barge-in, and it must clear the cessation floor's detect--decide--cease--resume decomposition \citep{garcia2026cessationfloor}.
The composition is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:compose}
  t_{k_{\mathrm{dec}}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{decide}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{cease}}
  \;<\;
  t_{\mathrm{escalation\text{-}only}},
\end{equation}
and only conversations satisfying \eqref{eq:compose} are ones where early prediction converts into an actionable intervention rather than a post-hoc annotation.
The cycle-length lemma of Section~\ref{sec:grammar} completes the picture from the other side: a run exceeding $k^{\ast}$ tool-loop cycles is off-path with high probability, so the stopping rule rarely has to wait past the horizon the grammar already justifies.

\subsection{False-Early-Exit Economics}
\label{subsec:economics}
Stopping early is only justified if the cost model says so.
Recall the banking cost $c(\hat{\theta}, \theta)$ over declared versus true terminal classes from Section~\ref{sec:grammar}.
False containment --- declaring \textit{contained} while the truth is \textit{handoff} or \textit{compliance\_escape} --- is the expensive error: it converts a recoverable failure into an unattended one, and a compliance escape has unbounded cost.
False escalation --- declaring \textit{handoff} while the truth is \textit{contained} --- wastes teller minutes, the trade that queue-level control already prices from the outside of the conversation \citep{queuecontrol2026}.
Abandonment adds a patience-driven cost that queueing models estimate from uncertain data \citep{silentabandonment2024}; the grammar lets us price it from the conversation's own trajectory.

\begin{proposition}[Cost-asymmetric boundaries]
\label{prop:asymmetric}
Let the cost model satisfy $c(\theta_0, \theta_a) > c(\theta_a, \theta_0)$ for every action class $a$, with $c(\theta_0, \textit{compliance\_escape})$ unbounded.
Then the error budgets that minimize expected cost satisfy
$\alpha_{\textit{compliance\_escape}} < \alpha_{\textit{handoff}} \ll \beta_{\textit{compliance\_escape}}$,
and the upper boundaries inherit the same ordering, $A_{\textit{compliance\_escape}} > A_{\textit{handoff}}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For a single-turn Bayes decision with posterior $p(\theta_a \mid x_{1:k})$, the optimal rule declares $\theta_a$ exactly when the posterior exceeds the cost ratio $\frac{c(\theta_a,\theta_0)}{c(\theta_a,\theta_0) + c(\theta_0,\theta_a)}$.
An unbounded $c(\theta_0, \textit{compliance\_escape})$ drives that ratio's complement toward zero, forcing the compliance boundary to demand far less evidence before firing and correspondingly more evidence before declaring contained.
The sequential rule inherits the ordering because its boundaries are the sequential realization of the same posterior-threshold test at every turn.
\end{proof}

The practical reading is that the four terminal classes do not share one threshold.
A banking monitor should fire its compliance escape at the first credible signal, accept a higher false-handoff rate as the price, and treat contained as a verdict that must be earned against every action hypothesis simultaneously.

\subsection{TRACER as the Fixed-Sample Instance}
\label{subsec:tracer}
The nearest published system to this decision layer is TRACER, which scores every visible dialogue prefix for eventual failure and fires a recovery trigger when the score exceeds a development-tuned threshold $\tau = 0.7788$, frozen under a false-positive-rate constraint of $0.15$ \citep{nourbakhsh2026tracer}.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-sample instance]
\label{cor:tracer}
TRACER's thresholded trigger is the fixed-sample instance of Definition~\ref{def:rule}: a single look at each prefix with a learned posterior, a symmetric boundary in place of \eqref{eq:boundaries}, and no optional stopping.
The Trajectory Stopping Theorem adds the three properties that a single-look trigger cannot express: anytime validity of the error guarantees under optional stopping, an expected decision-time bound \eqref{eq:times}, and cost-derived asymmetry across the four banking outcome classes instead of one binary failure label.
\end{corollary}

This is a generalization claim, not a criticism: TRACER's detected signals appear from the first quarter of the dialogue and its operating point is honest about its false-positive budget \citep{nourbakhsh2026tracer}.
What the sequential layer contributes is the freedom to stop looking, the price of that freedom in turns, and thresholds that differ per outcome class because the costs differ.
The evaluation protocol of Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} therefore uses TRACER's early-detection metrics with credit, extended with the cost-weighted decision-latency accounting that floor composition demands.
