# Review — Reviewer 2 (Domain Expert: Conversational AI / Voice Agents / Banking Contact Centers)

## Summary
The paper proposes a grammar over seven conversation states with four terminal banking outcome classes, a sequential stopping rule for early outcome prediction, a KPI-wired evaluation protocol on private Spanish banking call logs, and intervention coupling into a production orchestration graph. The positioning is unusually honest and well-verified: TRACER is described with its actual numbers and credited rather than strawmanned, and the four-strand taxonomy correctly identifies what benchmarks, process mining, forecasting, and queueing each leave open. The multi-channel observation model is the right design given the authors' own near-chance Spanish text-only turn-end result. However, the central formal object does not typecheck as specified: the declared transition alphabet cannot reach three of the four terminal classes, the cycle-depth definition contradicts its own intended semantics, and the flagship fraud signature is not expressible in the grammar as written.

## Score
**5 / 10 — Major Revision.**
As a domain piece, the framing is strong: the state-to-KPI wiring (§5.1) matches what a contact-center operator actually reports, the cost asymmetries (false containment worse than false handoff, compliance unbounded) are the correct operational reading, and the leave-one-campaign-out instinct plus the private-vs-synthetic release honesty (§5.7, §8) are better than typical. But a banking AI engineering team cannot deploy the paper's central artifact as specified: Table 1's alphabet produces well-formed runs ending only in `handoff` (and `contained` only via a figure edge the table does not declare), the drift statistic's definition counts the tool episode it explicitly wants to exempt, the auth-cycling fraud signature has no transition type to ride on, and the cost-asymmetry proposition's stated boundary ordering is contradicted by its own proof sketch. Every one of these is fixable with targeted edits, which is why this is major revision and not rejection.

## Mandatory revisions

1. **(P0) Three of four terminal classes are unreachable under δ — Table 1 and Definition 1, `sections/03_grammar.tex` (§3.1).** The typed alphabet (Table 1) contains exactly one transition targeting a terminal class: `escalation: any q_p → θ_hand`. `disclosure` targets `q_close ∈ Q_p`, and no transition type produces θ_cont, θ_aban, or θ_esc. Yet §3.1 defines θ_cont ("reaches a terminal through q_close"), θ_aban ("caller disconnects … surfaces as a hang-up event"), and θ_esc ("terminal confirmation without the guarded disclosure transition") — none derivable from δ as declared, so by Definition 2 (Run) these are not well-formed runs of G. Worse, θ_esc is defined as violating the *only* confirm→close edge the grammar declares, i.e., escape runs use an edge outside Σ by construction. **Fix:** add terminal transition types to Σ and Table 1 — e.g., `service-complete/teardown → θ_cont`, `hangup → θ_aban`, and either an `unguarded-close` regression type into θ_esc or an explicit audit-overlay definition of θ_esc outside the runs of G — and reconcile `figures/fig_grammar.tex`, which already invents the missing edges (`close` co→cont, `no-confirm` cl→aban, `defer` cl→hand, `policy breach` tl→comp) that Table 1 omits.

2. **(P1) Figure and grammar disagree on states and transitions — `figures/fig_grammar.tex`, `figures/fig_cycles.tex` vs. Definition 1.** Both figures draw a **six**-state spine (`gr, au, in, tl, cf, cl`) while Definition 1 and the abstract specify **seven** progress states; `q_clarify` has no node in either figure despite being the centerpiece of the cycle-depth discussion. Additionally, the figure's `tool-fail` edge goes `tl → in` while Table 1 specifies `tool-fail: q_tool → q_tool (retry)`, and the figure's `confirm→close` edge is labelled "confirm" while Table 1's guarded edge is `disclosure`. **Fix:** redraw the spine with all seven states and make every drawn edge a row of Table 1.

3. **(P1) Cycle-depth definition contradicts its stated semantics — Definition 3 (`def:cycledepth`) and Proposition 1's proof, `sections/03_grammar.tex` (§3.2).** The definition counts "a maximal sub-run that departs from a state q and later returns to q." Because `tool-call` is `q_tool → q_tool`, any tool episode literally departs from and returns to q_tool, so under the definition as written D(σ_L) ≥ 1 for the clean spine of Proposition 1's proof — contradicting both the proof's claim D(σ_L) = 0 and the text's "iterating tool-call inside q_tool is spine progress." Table 1's caption already states the intended rule ("regression types increment cycle depth"). **Fix:** redefine D over regression-typed transitions (excluding same-state iterations of progress types), then re-verify Proposition 1, Remark 1, and `fig_cycles.tex` (D = 3 panel) against the corrected definition.

4. **(P1) The fraud signature is not expressible in the declared grammar — §6.3 (`sections/06_intervention.tex`) vs. Table 1 (`sections/03_grammar.tex`).** §6.3 defines the social-engineering signature as "a trajectory that cycles on the auth state … across tool-fail and user-answer transitions without progress into intent." But Table 1 has no auth-failure/re-auth transition type: `user-answer` from q_auth is *progress* to "next spine state," and `tool-fail` lives in q_tool. (The figure's `au→au` "re-auth" loop is another edge with no table row.) The paper's flagship fraud pattern is therefore untypeable in G. **Fix:** add an `auth-fail` regression type (source→target q_auth→q_auth, emitter: auth service) to Table 1 and restate §6.3's D(σ) condition in terms of it.

5. **(P1) Proposition 2 (cost-asymmetric boundaries) states an ordering its own proof contradicts — `sections/04_stopping.tex` (§4.5).** With c(θ0, compliance_escape) unbounded, the proof sketch's Bayes threshold c(esc,cont)/(c(esc,cont)+c(cont,esc)) → 0, which implies *fire early*: β_esc → 0 (misses of the unbounded-cost class vanish) and a *lower* upper boundary A_esc < A_handoff. The proposition asserts the opposite chain — α_compliance < α_handoff ≪ β_compliance and A_compliance > A_handoff — i.e., a *large* miss budget for the class whose misses are unbounded and a compliance boundary that demands *more* evidence. This also contradicts the section's own prose ("fire its compliance escape at the first credible signal") and §6.3's conservative-fraud-flag stance. **Fix:** fix the α/β conventions, re-derive the ordering, and state one consistent stance on whether false compliance alarms or missed escapes dominate the design point.

6. **(P1) SPRT formulation precision — `sections/04_stopping.tex` (§4.1–4.3).** Four specific defects: (a) Eq. (1) defines the LLR over p(x_{1:k} | θ) — transcript only — while §4.1 and Definition 5 insist the posterior condition on the full multi-channel tuple o_k; the paper's own CD3 claim is undercut by its own notation — write p(o_{1:k} | θ) throughout. (b) k* is used for two different quantities: the cycle threshold max{k : Λ_k ≤ τ_resp} (Lemma 1, §3.2) and the expected-decision-time bound (abstract, Theorem 1, §5.3) — rename one. (c) "k*(δ, c)" in Theorem 1's closing sentence and the "confidence 1−δ" are never defined: for a one-vs-rest ensemble, state exactly what δ aggregates (e.g., union bound over per-class α_a under θ0 and β_a under θ_a) and under which mixture the expected time is taken. (d) The proof sketch calls the log-ratio "a martingale" under the true class; it is a sub/super-martingale with drift μ1/μ0 — state the drift form and how the excess-over-boundary term is bounded or absorbed.

7. **(P1) Leave-one-campaign-out is underspecified and leaks — `sections/05_evaluation.tex` (§5.2) and §7 (last paragraph).** The text conflates the fold unit: "Splits are campaign-disjoint: leave-one-campaign-out validation holds out each collection cycle" — a campaign (§7: card blocks, transfers, fraud reports) is an intent family; a collection cycle is a time window; these are different generalization claims. Further, if rare classes cluster in one campaign, some folds will contain zero compliance escapes, making class-conditional detection rates undefined; and repeat contacts (a cost the paper itself prices, §2.5/§6.4) put the same caller in train and test. **Fix:** name the fold unit explicitly, report per-fold per-class label counts, add a temporal split, and state the caller-level leakage policy.

8. **(P1) Abandonment mid-tool-loop is operationally under-defined — §3.1 (`sections/03_grammar.tex`) and §5.1 (`sections/05_evaluation.tex`).** In a real cascade, a hang-up during q_tool does not cancel in-flight tool side effects: a transfer may complete after teardown, so telephony KPIs may record a resolved need while the grammar records θ_aban ("disconnects before any terminal confirmation"). The class also does not distinguish caller BYE, network drop, and agent-initiated disconnect, which have different causes and different precursors. **Fix:** specify hang-up-cause disambiguation and the treatment of post-teardown tool completion in the terminal-class definitions and the KPI mapping.

9. **(P1) The social-engineering signature is asserted without evidence and inverts the common vishing pattern — §6.3 (`sections/06_intervention.tex`).** The claim that "a coerced caller cannot produce the credential the legitimate account holder knows" is uncited; in the dominant voice-fraud pattern the victim *is* the account holder and reads the OTP aloud, so authentication *succeeds* and this signature is blind. Repeated auth failure over Spanish telephony is confounded by ASR digit-string error, forgotten credentials, and accessibility — the likely majority base. The proposed action (step-up authentication, not lockout) is defensible; the "social-engineering signature" naming is not. **Fix:** cite fraud/vishing literature or production base rates, reframe as an anomaly trigger for step-up verification rather than a fraud verdict, and state the successful-auth blind spot explicitly.

10. **(P2, honesty) Conclusion overstates the release — `sections/09_conclusion.tex`.** "The Spanish trajectory benchmark and the frozen evaluation splits will be published on Hugging Face" contradicts §5.7/§8, which promise a *synthetic* mirror containing no personal data. Add "synthetic" — for a banking paper the difference between "the benchmark" and "a synthetic schema-identical mirror" is exactly the honesty this paper trades on.
