# Review — r4_banking_operations (Contact-Centre / Banking Operations)

## Summary
Sections 5–6 wire the four terminal classes (contained, abandoned, handoff, compliance-escape) to metrics operators genuinely track, and the escalation economics (agent-minutes / teller-minutes / customer patience) read as written by someone who has sat near a queue dashboard. The strongest ops choices are: compliance escape priced unbounded and reported separately; fraud-flag routed to step-up authentication rather than call termination; and the intervention window measured in wall-clock milliseconds including un-played TTS queue (§6.2), which is exactly where naive designs fail. The weak points are ground-truth provenance for compliance escape, a campaign-vs-cycle split ambiguity that invites leakage, and an equation/text contradiction in the cost-weighted metric.

## Score (1–10): 7
The KPI wiring is credible and the cost-asymmetry ordering (containment failure > handoff waste > compliance worst) matches how banking contact centres actually escalate: an unnecessary transfer is a staffing line item, a false containment is a relationship and repeat-contact problem, and a disclosure omission is a regulatory event. The decomposition of escalation into "queue control prices the teller, the grammar prices the drift" (§6.4) is the correct division of labour and composes with, rather than duplicates, existing queue-side tooling. It falls short of 8–9 because the protocol's hardest class — compliance escape — is measured on audit-sampled labels with no statement of the audit selection rule, the headline cost metric contradicts its own escape-exclusion rule, and the fraud-signature claim is asserted without the base-rate evidence that a fraud-ops reviewer would demand before letting a monitor fire step-up auth on "the first confident posterior."

## Mandatory revisions
1. **Compliance-escape ground truth is partially labelled and selection-biased (§5.1).** "Established by post-call audit" + audit sampling means positives exist only where audit looked; non-audited calls implicitly become compliant negatives. This poisons per-class precision/recall for exactly the rare, costly class the paper says carries the operational cost. Specify the audit selection rule (random vs risk-triggered), what fraction of calls is audited, and how unevaluated calls are treated (excluded, or negative-by-default with sensitivity analysis).
2. **Equation/text contradiction in the cost-weighted metric (§5.3).** Eq. (cost_weighted_latency) sums over all θ ∈ T including compliance escape, but the same paragraph says escape "is reported separately, never averaged into a scalar." Fix the sum (exclude θ_esc or replace its π_θ with an indicator that breaks the scalar) or fix the text; as written the headline metric is undefined at its most important class.
3. **Split axis ambiguity and seasonality leakage (§5.2).** "Campaign-disjoint: leave-one-campaign-out validation holds out each collection cycle" conflates two axes. In collections, adjacent cycles of the same campaign contain near-duplicate flows; holding out one cycle while training on the rest leaks seasonality structure. Define the axis precisely, justify LOCO as controlling drift (or add temporal blocking), and report per-campaign/per-cycle variance so prevalence shift across cycles is visible.
4. **Fraud signature claim and firing policy need evidence or softening (§6.3).** "A coerced caller cannot produce the credential the legitimate account holder knows" is asserted as *the* canonical signature, yet in account-takeover vishing the caller often holds (or is coached past) much of the knowledge check, while legitimate customers fail auth routinely — auth-failure cycling is dominated by benign forgetful users. Before a monitor is allowed to fire step-up auth "on the first confident posterior" under an unbounded-miss justification, the section must state the benign base rate of auth-failure cycles and the false-positive friction budget (step-up itself drives abandonment in legitimate calls).

## Optional suggestions
1. Price post-handoff queue abandonment into the false-handoff cost: a premature handoff can convert a containable call into an abandoned one via wait time (§5.3/§6.4).
2. Add 30-day repeat-contact rate as a downstream validation KPI for the false-containment pricing — it is the operational observable for the frustration/repeat-contact mechanism you invoke.
3. Report per-class prevalence alongside macro metrics; collections cycles shift delinquency mix, and macro averages hide it.
4. Consider average-handle-time / cost-per-contact as a fourth currency in §6.4; it is the number finance actually signs off on.

3. Does the false-handoff cost include queue wait and post-transfer abandonment, or only agent/teller minutes?
