**Summary of what I understood:** The paper claims — and I find the claim intact after fixing Table 1 — that no published work maps standard agent-observability exports (OTel GenAI, OpenInference, LangSmith run trees) into a discrete conversation-state space with terminal outcome classes and cycle depth, nor uses that representation to measure which regions of real conversational behavior a simulator or agent version covers vs misses. It ships a deliberately rule-based, config-exposed extraction τ whose output (walks) is the only contract downstream metrics depend on, then defines region coverage: real-anchored PCA to 2-D, Gaussian-bump density grid with a shared relative occupancy threshold, coverage overall and per outcome class, and top-K uncovered regions each returned with its nearest real exemplar as a scenario seed. The atlas is the visualization of this computation. The only executed evidence is a fixture-scale worked example (40.2% coverage, 1 sim conversation vs 2 real exports); §6 is an honest protocol (sensitivity sweep, class-conditional coverage, baseline ladder, transfer rank-correlation headline) with no fabricated results. The format characterizations driving the extraction are accurate to the specs; the weaknesses are editing artifacts (duplication, caption/equation mismatch) and an incomplete neighbor table, all fixable in text.
